Business Negotiations

Learn the language of professional negotiation — from opening positions to closing deals. Phrases for agreeing, disagreeing, making concessions, and reaching agreement.

Warm-Up

What Is Negotiation?

Discuss these questions, then reveal the answers.

Negotiation = Discussion Aimed at Agreement
A negotiation is a conversation between two or more parties who each want something, and who need to reach an agreement that everyone can accept.
1. What is the difference between a negotiation and an argument?
In an argument, each side tries to win. In a negotiation, each side tries to find a solution both can accept. The goal is not to defeat the other person — it is to reach a deal.
2. What happens if one side gives in too easily?
They lose value. Good negotiation means understanding what you need, what the other side needs, and finding the overlap. Giving in too early often means leaving value on the table.

Key Phrases

Opening a Negotiation

State your position
"We are looking for a 10% reduction in price."
Clear, direct, no apology.
Show flexibility
"We are open to discussing the terms."
Signals willingness to move.
Ask for their view
"What are your expectations on this?"
Shows respect, gathers information.
Set the tone
"We hope we can find a solution that works for both of us."
Collaborative, not aggressive.
The opening sets the emotional tone. Start collaborative, not aggressive. Even in hard negotiations, phrases like "We hope we can find a solution" keep the relationship intact.
Key Phrases

Disagreeing Without Damaging the Relationship

Soft disagreement
"I see your point, but we have some concerns."
Acknowledges their view before yours.
Delaying
"We would need to look at that more closely."
Buys time without saying no.
Conditional
"We could consider that if..."
Makes agreement dependent on something.
Firm boundary
"That is not something we can agree to."
Clear, final, still professional.
In international business, direct "no" can feel rude. Use "I see your point, but..." or "We would need to look at that more closely." These phrases disagree without closing the door.
Key Phrases

Making Concessions

Trade-off
"We could reduce the price if you commit to a longer contract."
Give something, get something.
Small concession
"We can offer a 5% discount as a gesture of goodwill."
Shows flexibility without giving away too much.
Conditional offer
"If you can confirm by Friday, we can include delivery."
Creates urgency, rewards action.
Final position
"That is the best we can do."
Signals the limit has been reached.
Never give something for nothing. Every concession should be conditional: "We could do X if you can do Y." This protects your value and keeps the negotiation moving.

Key Phrases

Closing the Deal

Summarise agreement
"So we have agreed on X, Y, and Z. Shall we confirm in writing?"
Prevents misunderstanding later.
Next steps
"We will send the contract by Tuesday."
Keeps momentum after the meeting.
Positive close
"We are pleased to have reached an agreement."
Reinforces the relationship.
Relationship close
"We look forward to working together."
Focuses on the future, not just the deal.
Always summarise what was agreed before leaving. "So we have agreed on..." prevents the "I thought you meant..." problem later. Then confirm in writing within 24 hours.

2. Role-play: You are negotiating the price of a service. Write the dialogue — your opening, their pushback, your concession, and the close.
