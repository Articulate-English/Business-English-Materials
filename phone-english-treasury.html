Phone English for Treasury

Professional telephone skills for treasury and supply — calling customers, suppliers and partners with confidence and clarity.

Warm-Up

Warm-Up — Phone Confidence

Discuss these questions, then reveal the model answers.

1. What is the most difficult part of a business phone call for you?
Many people find it hard to understand fast speakers, to explain numbers clearly, or to handle unexpected questions without visual cues. The good news is that all of these skills improve with practice and a few reliable phrases.
2. Why are phone skills especially important in treasury and supply roles?
Treasury specialists discuss payments, invoices, delivery schedules and contracts — often with people in different time zones. A clear, professional phone call prevents costly misunderstandings and builds trust with suppliers and customers.
3. What is the difference between a good phone call and a bad one?
A good call has a clear purpose, a polite opening, confirmed details and a clear next step. A bad call lacks structure — the caller rambles, forgets key information, and both parties hang up unsure of what was agreed.
4. What should you prepare before making an important business call?
Have your notes ready: the person's name and department, the purpose of the call, any invoice or reference numbers, and the questions you need to ask. A short written agenda keeps you focused and confident.
Useful phrases:
Good morning, this is [name] from [company].I'm calling about...Could you please confirm...?Let me read that back to you.Thank you for your time.

A Real Phone Call

Read this phone call transcript. What is the caller trying to achieve? How does the structure help?

Phone Call: Payment Confirmation
Caller: Nikos Papadopoulos, Treasury Specialist, Motor Oil Hellas SA  ·  Recipient: Hans Mueller, Accounts, Brenntag GmbH  ·  Date: 15 September 2026
Opening — "Good morning, this is Nikos Papadopoulos from Motor Oil Hellas SA. Could I speak to Hans Mueller in Accounts, please?"
Purpose — "I'm calling about invoice 4712, which was due on 10 September. Our records show it is still outstanding. Could you please confirm whether the payment has been processed?"
Clarification — "I see. And do you have a reference number for the remittance? ... Let me read that back to you: reference MOT-0926-4712. Is that correct?"
Resolution — "Thank you, Hans. So the payment was made on 12 September and should reach us within three business days. I will check again on Thursday and call you if it has not arrived."
Closing — "Thank you for your time, Hans. Have a good afternoon. Goodbye."
The caller wants to confirm whether an overdue invoice has been paid. The structure helps because every stage has one clear job: the opening identifies the caller, the purpose states the reason, the clarification confirms the details, the resolution agrees the next step, and the closing ends politely. Both parties hang up knowing exactly what will happen next.
Key Component 1

Opening the Call

What it is: The first 15 seconds of the call — greeting, your name, your company, and the request to speak to someone.

Why it matters: A clear, confident opening tells the listener exactly who you are and why you are calling. It sets a professional tone and saves time.

Example: "Good morning, this is Nikos Papadopoulos from Motor Oil Hellas SA. Could I speak to Hans Mueller in Accounts, please?"

Always say your name and company first — do not wait for the other person to ask. If you are not sure who answered, ask politely: "Is that the Accounts department?" A strong opening gives you control of the call from the first second.
Key Component 2

Stating the Purpose

What it is: A short, clear sentence that explains why you are calling — with any reference numbers or dates.

Why it matters: The listener needs to understand the purpose immediately. A vague purpose ("I wanted to ask about something") wastes time and sounds unprofessional.

Example: "I'm calling about invoice 4712, which was due on 10 September. Our records show it is still outstanding."

Use the pattern: "I'm calling about [topic] + [specific detail]." This tells the listener exactly what to prepare. If you have an invoice number, a delivery date, or a contract reference, state it here — do not make the other person search for it.
Key Component 3

Clarifying Details

What it is: Asking for confirmation of numbers, dates, names, or reference codes — and reading them back to check.

Why it matters: On the phone, you cannot see the other person's screen. A misheard number or date can cause a payment to go to the wrong account or a delivery to arrive late.

Example: "Let me read that back to you: reference MOT-0926-4712. Is that correct?"

Always read back numbers, dates and reference codes. Use the pattern: "Let me read that back to you: [detail]. Is that correct?" This takes five seconds and prevents expensive mistakes. If the other person says "no", ask them to spell it or say it slowly.
Key Component 4

Handling Problems on the Phone

What it is: What to say when something goes wrong — a late payment, a missing delivery, a disagreement, or a misunderstanding.

Why it matters: Problems are stressful, especially in a second language. Having ready-made phrases keeps you calm and professional.

Example: "I understand, but according to our records the payment is still outstanding. Could you please check again and call me back by tomorrow?"

Stay calm and polite. Acknowledge the other person's point first ("I understand"), then state the facts ("according to our records..."), then request a specific action ("could you please call me back by tomorrow?"). Never blame or raise your voice — it damages the relationship.
Key Component 5

Agreeing the Next Step

What it is: Before ending the call, both parties agree on what will happen next — who will do what, and by when.

Why it matters: Without a clear next step, the call achieves nothing. Both people hang up hoping the other person will act — and nothing happens.

Example: "So I will check again on Thursday and call you if the payment has not arrived. Is that agreeable?"

Use the pattern: "So I will [action] by [date]. Is that agreeable?" This makes the next step explicit and gives the other person a chance to object. A call without a clear next step is a call wasted.
Key Component 6

Closing the Call

What it is: The final seconds — thanking the person, saying goodbye, and ending the call politely.

Why it matters: The closing is the last impression you leave. A polite, confident closing reinforces the professional relationship.

Example: "Thank you for your time, Hans. Have a good afternoon. Goodbye."

Always thank the other person by name. Do not rush the closing — it feels abrupt and rude. A good closing sounds like: "Thank you for your time, [name]. [Polite wish]. Goodbye." The extra two seconds cost nothing and leave a lasting positive impression.
Analysis

What Makes a Good Phone Call?

A strong business phone call passes these six tests. Which ones would your calls pass?

Clear purpose — the listener knows why you are calling within 15 seconds.
Prepared — you have your notes, reference numbers and questions ready.
Structured — opening, purpose, clarification, next step and closing.
Confirmed — numbers and dates are read back and checked.
Calm — problems are handled politely, without blame or raised voices.
Actionable — both parties know exactly what will happen next.
Test every call with two questions: "Did the listener know my purpose?" and "Do both parties know the next step?". If a call cannot answer both, it was not finished — it was abandoned. And remember: on the phone, your voice is your only tool. Speak clearly, smile while you talk, and the listener will hear the confidence in your voice.
Practice

Weak vs Strong

Improve these weak phone phrases.

1. Weak: "Hello, is this Hans? I need to talk about the money." — What makes this weak?
It has no company name, no clear purpose, no reference number, and the word "money" is too vague. The listener does not know who is calling, why, or what action is needed.
2. Rewrite: "Can you send the payment soon?"
"Could you please process the payment for invoice 4712 by Friday, 19 September? Let me know if that is possible."
3. Which components are missing here? "Hi Hans, it's Nikos. The invoice is late. Call me back."
The purpose is vague (which invoice?), there is no reference number, no clarification, no agreed next step, and the closing is abrupt. The listener has no information and no clear instruction.
