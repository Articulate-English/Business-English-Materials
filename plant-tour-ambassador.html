Warm-Up

Warm-Up — The Plant Tour

Discuss these questions, then reveal the model answers.

1. Have you ever given a tour of your workplace to a visitor? How did it feel?
Many people feel nervous at first — especially when the visitors are senior partners from another country. The key is preparation: know your process, plan your route, and practise your key phrases until they feel natural.
2. What makes visitors feel welcome during a plant tour?
A warm greeting, clear explanations, attention to safety, and genuine enthusiasm for the work. Visitors remember how you made them feel — not just what they saw.
3. Why is safety language important when guiding non-experts?
Visitors may not know the hazards. Clear, confident safety instructions protect them and show that your company takes responsibility seriously. It builds trust.
4. How do you explain a technical process to someone who is not an engineer?
Use simple comparisons, avoid jargon, focus on the purpose and the result, and check understanding. A good tour guide makes complex processes feel accessible — not overwhelming.
Useful phrases:
Welcome to our facility.If you'd like to follow me…The key thing to notice here is…I'd like to draw your attention to…We take great pride in…

A Real Plant Tour Script

Read this tour script from ItalChimica S.p.A. Notice how the guide moves from welcome to closing — and how each section has a clear purpose.

ItalChimica S.p.A. — Hydrogen Production Plant Tour
Guide: Plant Tour Ambassador  ·  Visitors: US & UK Partners  ·  Duration: 45 minutes
1. Welcome & Safety Briefing — "Welcome to ItalChimica. My name is [Name], and I'll be your guide today. Before we begin, please ensure your safety glasses and helmets are secured. The tour will take approximately 45 minutes, and I'll explain each stage of our hydrogen production process. If you have any questions at any point, please don't hesitate to ask."
2. Electrolysis Hall — "This is our electrolysis hall. We split water into hydrogen and oxygen using a controlled electric current. The hydrogen gas you see collecting at the cathode is 99.9% pure. We process 500 cubic metres per hour. The key thing to notice here is the membrane in the centre — it allows ions to pass through but keeps the two gases completely separate."
3. Purification & Compression — "The hydrogen then passes through our purification unit, where any remaining moisture and oxygen are removed. After purification, the gas is compressed to 20 bar for the next stage. I'd like to draw your attention to the pressure gauges on the left — they're monitored continuously by our control room."
4. Liquefaction Cold Box — "This is our cold box, where hydrogen gas is cooled from ambient temperature down to minus 253 degrees Celsius — that's just 20 degrees above absolute zero. At this temperature, hydrogen condenses into a liquid. The process uses liquid nitrogen for precooling to 80 Kelvin, and expansion turbines for the final cooling stage. Rest assured that the entire system is vacuum-insulated for safety and efficiency."
5. Storage & Loading — "The liquid hydrogen is stored in vacuum-insulated tanks at atmospheric pressure. From here, it's loaded onto cryogenic tankers for transport. Our storage capacity is 200 tonnes. We take great pride in our safety record — we've had zero incidents in over 10 years of operation."
6. Closing & Q&A — "Thank you for visiting ItalChimica today. I hope this tour has given you a clear understanding of our production capabilities and our commitment to safety. I'd be happy to answer any questions you may have about our processes, our standards, or our future projects. Before you leave, please help yourselves to refreshments in the reception area."
The script follows a clear structure: welcome and safety first, then each production stage in order (electrolysis → purification → liquefaction → storage), and a warm closing with Q&A. Each section has a purpose: inform, demonstrate expertise, reassure about safety, and build trust. The guide uses confident, professional language throughout — and always directs attention to what matters most.
Key Component 1

Opening the Tour

What it is: The first two minutes of your tour — greeting visitors, setting the agenda, and establishing your credibility as a guide.

Why it matters: First impressions are powerful. A confident opening tells visitors they are in safe hands and that the tour will be worth their time.

Example: "Welcome to ItalChimica. My name is [Name], and I'll be your guide today. We'll visit four areas of the plant, and I'll explain each stage of the process. The tour takes about 45 minutes."

Always open with three things: your name, the tour route, and the time. This gives visitors a mental map and reduces anxiety. Smile, make eye contact, and speak slightly slower than normal — especially with international guests who may need a moment to process.
Key Component 2

Describing Processes

What it is: Explaining how a process works — step by step — in clear, accessible language that non-experts can follow.

Why it matters: Visitors remember the story of the process, not the technical details. A clear explanation shows mastery and builds confidence in your operation.

Example: "First, water enters the electrolyser. Then, an electric current splits it into hydrogen and oxygen. The hydrogen collects at the cathode, and the oxygen collects at the anode. Once this is complete, the hydrogen moves to the purification unit."

Use sequencing phrases: first, then, next, once this is complete, the next stage is, finally. Avoid jargon — or if you must use a technical term, explain it immediately. "This is the cathode — the negative electrode where hydrogen gas collects."
Key Component 3

Safety & Compliance Language

What it is: The phrases you use to keep visitors safe, reassure them, and show that your company meets international standards.

Why it matters: Safety language is not optional — it is a legal and moral requirement. Clear, confident safety instructions protect visitors and demonstrate professionalism.

Example: "Please keep to the marked walkway at all times. If you hear the alarm, follow me immediately to the assembly point. Rest assured that this is a standard procedure — we practise it every month."

Use please and rest assured to sound firm but polite. Never joke about safety — visitors need to trust that you take it seriously. Mention your safety record if it is strong: "We've operated without incident for over 10 years."
Key Component 4

Being a Company Ambassador

What it is: Representing your company with pride, handling difficult questions, and building trust with international partners.

Why it matters: You are not just explaining a process — you are showing visitors that your company is professional, reliable, and worth doing business with.

Example: "We take great pride in our environmental standards. Our electrolysis process produces zero carbon emissions — the only byproduct is oxygen, which we capture and supply to local hospitals."

Pride is professional. Saying "we take great pride in" is stronger than "we are proud of" — it sounds more formal and more confident. If you don't know the answer to a question, say: "That's an excellent question — let me find the right person to answer that for you."
Key Component 5

The Diagram Slides

Use these diagrams to help visitors visualise the processes you are describing. Point to each stage as you explain it.

Diagram A: Electrolysis Cell — Water (H2O) is split by an electric current. Hydrogen (H2) collects at the cathode (-), oxygen (O2) at the anode (+). The membrane keeps the gases separate.
Diagram B: Hydrogen Liquefaction Process — Feed gas is purified, compressed, then cooled in two stages (80K precool, 20K deep cool) before passing through a Joule-Thomson valve into the storage tank as liquid hydrogen.
Key Component 6

Closing & Follow-Up

What it is: The final minutes of your tour — summarising the visit, thanking partners, and setting up the next step.

Why it matters: A strong closing leaves visitors feeling positive and professional. It is also the perfect moment to reinforce your key message and agree on next steps.

Example: "Thank you for visiting ItalChimica today. I hope this tour has given you confidence in our production capabilities. I'll send you the technical specifications by Thursday, and we can schedule a follow-up call whenever suits you."

Always end with three things: a thank-you, a summary of what they saw, and a clear next step. Follow up within 24 hours with a brief email — it shows professionalism and keeps the relationship warm.
Analysis

What Makes a Plant Tour Great?

A great plant tour passes these six tests. Which ones would your tour pass?

Prepared — you know the route, the key points, and the answers to likely questions.
Safe — visitors are protected, informed, and confident throughout.
Clear — every process is explained in accessible language, with no unexplained jargon.
Confident — you speak with authority and genuine pride in your work.
Professional — you represent your company with warmth and competence.
Memorable — visitors leave with a clear story they can tell others.
Test your tour with one question: "If I were a visitor from another country, would I feel welcome, safe, and impressed?" If the answer is yes, you are ready. Practise aloud at least three times before the real tour — confidence comes from repetition.
Practice

Weak vs Strong

Improve these weak tour phrases.

1. Weak: "This is a machine. It does stuff." — What makes this weak?
It is vague, unprofessional, and gives the visitor no useful information. It suggests the guide does not understand the process — or does not care to explain it.
2. Rewrite: "This is the cold box. It's very cold." — Make this stronger.
"This is our cold box, where hydrogen gas is cooled to minus 253 degrees Celsius — just 20 degrees above absolute zero. At this temperature, the gas condenses into a liquid, which is far easier to store and transport."
3. Which elements are missing here? "That's the storage area. Any questions?"
The capacity, the safety features, the purpose, and the pride are all missing. The visitor learns nothing and the guide misses an opportunity to demonstrate expertise. A strong closing adds value: "Our storage capacity is 200 tonnes, and we've maintained zero incidents in over 10 years of operation."

Homework

Include: (1) a welcome and safety briefing, (2) an opening that sets the agenda, (3) a clear description of the process using sequencing phrases, (4) a diagram with labels, (5) a section showing company pride, and (6) a closing with next steps. Practise aloud at least three times. Bring the script to class ready to present in five minutes.
