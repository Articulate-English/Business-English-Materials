Business English B2

Business Email Writing

Write clear, professional emails that get results. Learn the structure, phrases, and cultural rules for business email — plus how to adjust your tone for different situations.

Warm-Up

Why Does Email Matter?

Discuss these questions, then reveal the answers.

Email = Your Professional Reputation
In international business, your email is often the first impression you make. A clear, well-structured email signals competence. A confusing or overly casual one signals the opposite.
1. How is business email different from personal email?
Business email is more structured, more formal, and more purpose-driven. It has a clear subject line, a logical structure, and a specific call to action. Personal email can wander — business email should not.
2. What is the most common mistake non-native speakers make in business email?
Being too formal or too informal. Non-native speakers often swing between "Dear Sir/Madam, I am writing to inform you..." (too stiff) and "Hey, just wanted to check..." (too casual). The right tone is professional but natural.

Key Structure

The Five-Part Email

Subject line
"Re: Q3 Results — Action Required by Friday"
Clear, specific, includes deadline if relevant.
Opening
"I hope this email finds you well."
Brief, professional greeting.
Purpose
"I am writing to..."
State why you are writing in one sentence.
Details
"Specifically, I need..."
Bullet points make this scannable.
Call to action
"Could you please confirm by Wednesday?"
Clear next step, clear deadline.
Close
"Thank you in advance. Best regards,"
Polite, professional, ends the email.
Busy people scan emails in under 30 seconds. If your purpose and call to action are not in the first three lines, they may be missed. Use bullet points — walls of text get skipped.
Key Phrases

Casual vs Semi-Formal vs Formal Email

The level of formality depends on who you are writing to and your relationship with them. With colleagues, it can be less formal in some situations.

Formal
"Dear Ms. Chen, I am writing to inquire whether... I look forward to your response. Yours sincerely,"
For: senior executives, clients you do not know, official requests, first contact.
Semi-Formal
"Dear Maria, I wanted to check on the status of... Could you please let me know? Best regards,"
For: colleagues at the same level, people you have met, professional but friendly.
Casual
"Hi Maria, Just wanted to check if you had a chance to look at... Let me know! Thanks,"
For: close colleagues, people you know well, quick updates between teammates.
When in doubt
"Dear Maria, I hope you are well. I am writing to..."
Start semi-formal. You can always relax later if they reply casually.
With colleagues, the same person might get a formal email about a client issue and a casual email about lunch. Match the situation: formal for serious topics, casual for everyday chat. When in doubt, start semi-formal — it is professional without being stiff.
Key Phrases

Adjusting Your Tone

Formal
"I am writing to inquire whether..."
For senior people, clients, first contact.
Neutral
"I wanted to check on the status of..."
For colleagues you know professionally.
Direct
"Please send the report by Tuesday."
For people who report to you, or close colleagues.
Friendly
"Thanks so much for your help with this!"
For people you know well, after the work is done.
The rule: match the tone of the person who wrote to you. If a client writes formally, do not reply with "Hey!" If a colleague writes casually, do not reply with "Dear Sir/Madam."
Key Phrases

Common Email Situations

Making a request
"Would it be possible to...?"
Polite, not demanding.
Declining politely
"Thank you for the invitation, but unfortunately..."
Gracious, brief reason, no over-explaining.
Apologising
"I apologise for the delay in getting back to you."
Acknowledge, explain briefly, move on.
Chasing
"I just wanted to follow up on my email below."
Assume they are busy, not ignoring you.
When chasing someone, do not write "I am still waiting." That sounds passive-aggressive. Use "I just wanted to follow up..." — it assumes they are busy, not ignoring you.

Key Phrases

Email Etiquette Rules

Reply all?
Only if everyone truly needs to know.
"Reply all" is the #1 email annoyance.
Response time
Within 24 hours, even if just to acknowledge.
"Got it — I will get back to you by Thursday" is enough.
CC
"For information only" — no action needed.
Do not CC someone's boss to pressure them.
Forward
Add a brief note explaining why you are forwarding.
"FYI — see below" is lazy.
The golden rule of business email: every email should have a clear purpose. If you cannot say why you are sending it in one sentence, do not send it. Respect people's time.
