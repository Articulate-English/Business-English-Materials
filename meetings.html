Business Meetings

Run and participate in meetings with confidence. Learn the language for opening, managing discussion, asking for clarification, and agreeing actions — plus the cultural rules international students often miss.

Warm-Up

Why Do Meetings Matter?

Discuss these questions, then reveal the answers.

A Meeting = Purposeful Conversation
A meeting is a structured discussion with a goal: to share information, make a decision, or solve a problem. The key skill is not speaking — it is knowing when and how to speak.
1. Why do international professionals find English meetings difficult, even with good English?
Because meetings are not just about language — they are about cultural rules. Who speaks first, how to disagree, when to interrupt, and what "interesting" really means vary by culture.
2. What is the most common mistake non-native speakers make in meetings?
Staying silent. Many wait until they can say something perfect — but the meeting moves on. Short, clear contributions are better than long, perfect ones.

Key Phrases

Opening a Meeting

Welcome
"Thank you all for being here today."
Simple, warm, sets a positive tone.
State purpose
"The purpose of this meeting is to..."
Everyone knows why they are here.
Review agenda
"On the agenda today, we have..."
Gives structure, manages expectations.
Set time
"We have 45 minutes, so let us stay focused."
Respects everyone's time.
The first two minutes set the tone for the whole meeting. Always state the purpose and the agenda — without this, people wonder why they are there and the meeting drifts.
Key Phrases

Managing Discussion

Invite input
"What are your thoughts on this, Maria?"
Draws quieter people in by name.
Keep on track
"That is an important point — can we come back to the agenda?"
Respectful redirection.
Summarise
"So what I am hearing is..."
Shows you were listening, clarifies.
Parking lot
"Let us put that in the parking lot for now."
Acknowledges without derailing.
The biggest meeting killer is the person who talks too much. If someone is going long, interrupt politely: "Sorry to interrupt — can we capture that and come back to the main point?"
Key Phrases

Asking for Clarification

Check understanding
"Do you mean that...?"
Paraphrases to confirm.
Ask for example
"Could you give us a concrete example?"
Moves from abstract to specific.
Slow down
"Sorry, could you go over that last point again?"
Everyone needs to catch up sometimes.
Admit confusion
"I am not sure I follow — could you clarify?"
Better to ask than to guess.
Non-native speakers often stay silent when they do not understand — then struggle later. Asking "Could you clarify?" or "Do you mean that...?" is professional, not weak. Native speakers do it constantly.

Key Phrases

Closing a Meeting

Summarise decisions
"To summarise: we have decided on X, Y, and Z."
Everyone leaves with the same understanding.
Assign actions
"Maria, could you take the lead on that?"
Clear ownership, clear deadlines.
Confirm next steps
"We will reconvene next Tuesday at 10."
Keeps momentum between meetings.
Close
"Thank you all. That concludes today's meeting."
Signals the meeting is over.
Always end with three things: what was decided, who will do what by when, and when you meet next. Without this, the meeting was just a conversation — nothing happens afterward.
