Business English B2

Phrasal Verbs for Consultants

Forty essential phrasal verbs you will actually use in meetings, with clients, and in the small talk between meetings. Plus the rules for when they are and are not appropriate in formal English.

Warm-Up

What Is a Phrasal Verb?

Read the definition, then test yourself before revealing the answers.

Verb + Particle(s) = New Meaning
A phrasal verb is a verb combined with one or two small words (particles) — up, out, off, on, back, down, into, along — to create a meaning that is often very different from the original verb.
1. "The team turned around the project in six weeks." What does turned around mean here?
Transformed the project from failing to succeeding. The verb turn means to rotate; turn around means to reverse a situation. The particle completely changes the meaning.
2. Why does English have so many phrasal verbs instead of just using one-word Latin-based verbs like transform?
Phrasal verbs come from Old English and everyday speech; Latin-based verbs like transform entered English through writing and formal language centuries later. Both systems survived. Today, phrasal verbs dominate spoken English, while Latin-based verbs dominate formal documents.
Foundation

Three Types of Phrasal Verbs

Type 1: Intransitive
No object follows. "We need to catch up."
break down, show up, catch up, drop by
Type 2: Separable
Object can go between verb and particle. "Fill the form out" or "Fill out the form."
fill out, put off, point out, lay out
Type 3: Inseparable
Object must come after the particle. "Come up with a plan" never "Come a plan up with."
come up with, carry out, rule out, look into
Type 4: Three-part
Verb + two particles, always inseparable. "I came up with a solution."
come up with, get along with, look forward to
A practical shortcut: if you can put a pronoun between the verb and particle ("fill it out"), it is separable. If not, it is inseparable. When in doubt, keep the phrase together — it is almost always grammatically safe.
Key Concept

Why Most Phrasal Verbs Are Not Formal

This is the single most important rule for consultants.

Three reasons phrasal verbs feel informal:
1. Everyday origin. They grew out of spoken English. "Sort out", "fix up", "put off" belong to the kitchen and workshop first, the boardroom second.

2. Ambiguity. "Put off" can mean postpone, discourage, distract, or leave for later. Formal English prefers precision.

3. Tone. They sound like conversation, not analysis. "We looked into the issue" sounds like a chat; "We investigated the issue" sounds like a finding.
In formal documents — proposals, contracts, board reports, client emails — choose the single-word Latin verb.
We investigated the issue. We postponed the launch. We established the framework. We increased capacity. These are clearer and carry more authority in writing.
Key Concept

Which Phrasal Verbs ARE Acceptable in Formal English

Some phrasal verbs have been used in formal writing for so long that they lost their informal feel.

set up formal
to establish, to arrange"We set up a new office in Zurich."
carry out formal
to execute, to conduct"We carried out a review of processes."
bring about formal
to cause to happen"The change brought about significant savings."
lay out formal
to explain in a clear order"The proposal lays out three options."
point out formal
to highlight"The report points out several risks."
account for formal
to explain or justify"Can you account for the delay?"
rule out formal
to eliminate as a possibility"We cannot rule out further changes."
phase in formal
to introduce gradually"We phased in the new software over six weeks."
These phrasal verbs feel neutral because they have been used in government, law, and business writing for centuries. When a client reads "carry out", they do not think "casual" — they think "professional". Use them freely in both speech and writing.

Group 1: Project Delivery

Eight Phrasal Verbs for Getting Things Done

set up formal
to establish, arrange, or prepare"We set up the project team in two weeks."
carry out formal
to execute, conduct, perform"We carried out a full process audit."
roll out formal
to launch or introduce"We roll out the new system in March."
turn around informal
to transform from failing to succeeding"They turned the business around in one year."
phase out formal
to gradually stop using something"We phase out the legacy software by June."
scale up formal
to increase size, capacity, or volume"We need to scale up the team for Q3."
follow up formal
to check on progress after initial action"Let me follow up after the client call."
follow through informal
to complete what you started"The plan works if we follow through."

Group 3: Communication and Meetings

Eight Phrasal Verbs for Meetings

bring up informal
to mention, raise a topic"Can I bring up the budget issue?"
lay out formal
to present clearly in order"She laid out the proposal in ten slides."
spell out informal
to explain explicitly, leave no doubt"Let me spell out the risks for you."
call off informal
to cancel"We had to call off the client meeting."
put off informal
to postpone, delay"Let us put off the decision until May."
go over informal

Group 4: Progress and Performance

Eight Phrasal Verbs for Delivering Results

wind down informal
to reduce activity gradually, approach an end"We wind down the project in December."
ramp up informal
to increase effort or production"We ramp up hiring before the launch."
fall behind formal
to progress less quickly than expected"We are two weeks behind schedule."
catch up informal
to reach the same level as others"We need to catch up after the holidays."
keep up formal
to maintain pace, stay at the same level"The team kept up a strong pace all month."
pull off informal
to succeed at something difficult"They pulled off the migration on time."
push back formal
to postpone, or to resist a request"We pushed back the deadline by two weeks."
opt out formal
to choose not to participate"Two clients opted out of the pilot."

Group 5: Small Talk and Social English

Eight Phrasal Verbs for the Coffee Break

catch up (with) informal
to talk with someone you have not seen recently"Let us catch up after the conference."
get along (with) informal
to have a good relationship with someone"I get along well with the team in Basel."
fit in informal
to belong, be accepted by a group"She fitted in from the first day."
drop by informal
to visit informally"Feel free to drop by my office."
meet up informal
to meet someone, usually socially"Let us meet up after work on Thursday."
hang out informal
to spend time together casually"We hung out at the industry event."
bond over informal
to connect through a shared interest"We bonded over a love of hiking."
open up informal
to become more talkative or personal"He opened up about his career change."
Small talk phrasal verbs are almost all informal — which is exactly right. Using "catch up", "drop by", and "get along" makes you sound warm and natural. Match the register to the situation.

Study Skills

How to Remember 40 Phrasal Verbs

1. Group by particle
Up often means completion or increase: fill up, scale up, ramp up, speak up. Out often means outward, complete, or exclude: roll out, phase out, point out, rule out.
Once you see the pattern, you can guess meanings you have not memorised.
2. Group by context
Project delivery verbs. Meeting verbs. Small talk verbs. Your brain remembers situations, not isolated words.
Before a meeting, mentally rehearse the meeting verbs you want to use.
3. Visual story method
Create a vivid, personal story for each verb. "I pulled off the presentation by laying out three options, then summing up."
The more personal and absurd the image, the better you remember it.
4. Spaced repetition, one group per week
Week 1: Project delivery. Week 2: Analysis. Week 3: Meetings. Week 4: Progress. Week 5: Small talk. Review all on Week 6.
Five verbs per day for six days, then a review on day seven. One week per group.
